# Adversarial integration audit of the focused manuscript

**Manuscript audited:** `main.tex`, `references.bib`, and every file under
`sections/` in this directory.

**Audit posture:** every normalization, imported hypothesis, limit passage,
and nondegeneracy assertion was treated as untrusted. I reconstructed the
elementary race calculation, the hard-edge scaling, the shared-phase
Wasserstein bounds, the projection-DPP variance formula, and the heat bridge.
I also checked the relevant primary-source statements in Kowalski's two papers,
his published erratum, and Katz--Sarnak, rather than relying only on the
project's earlier notes.

**Bottom-line verdict:** I did **not** find a fatal mathematical contradiction
in the headline simultaneous quenched theorem. The proof architecture closes
after the imported Katz--Sarnak and Kowalski results are granted, and the key
normalizations in the current files are mutually consistent. This is a much
stronger conclusion than “the idea looks plausible,” but it is not a
submission certificate. There are several high-priority repairs, especially
the exceptional-set definition of the “actual endpoint law,” source-level
clarifications in the arithmetic-to-Haar transfer, and the absence of any TeX
build. A current specialist literature audit and external expert reading also
remain indispensable.

## 1. Fatal blockers

### F0. No fatal theorem-level blocker found in the current proof

I found no sign error, missing order-one term, false topology change, or
incompatible scaling that destroys Theorem 1.1. In particular:

- the prime-square term has the stated positive sign and gives the two parity
  centers;
- exponents at least three vanish for the entire range \(M=m+1/2>0\);
- the maximal-Weyl-group relation theorem does imply angle-plus-π linear
  independence after the square-field normalization;
- the Katz--Sarnak character estimate can be made genus-explicit;
- the heat bridge uses \(K_{2s}(e)\), not \(K_s(e)\), with Haar probability
  measure and the correct group dimension;
- the Haar eigenangle process has the stated symplectic hard-edge kernel under
  the scale \(d\vartheta/(2\pi)\), \(d=2g+1\);
- the coefficient tails are uniformly square summable, which is exactly the
  extra input needed to upgrade local point-process convergence to a random
  \(\mathcal P_2\)-law in \(W_2\);
- the random conditional measure and its positive-half-line mass are proved
  nonconstant by two logically distinct arguments.

This “no fatal blocker” finding is conditional on the cited deep arithmetic
theorems. It does not mean those theorems have been formalized in Lean, nor
does it replace expert checking of their application.

## 2. High-priority repairable gaps

### R1. The object called the “actual endpoint law” is modified on the bad set

**Location:** `sections/00-introduction.tex`, around the definition of
\(\nu_{g,t,\lambda}\), and `sections/05-assembly.tex`, first paragraph.

The introduction says that on the exceptional non-LI set one defines
\(\nu_{g,t,\lambda}=\delta_0\). The assembly then calls this the “actual
endpoint law,” and Theorem 1.1 calls its positive-half-line mass the natural
race density. Those statements are not literally compatible: on a bad
parameter, \(\delta_0\) need not be the empirical endpoint law and its
positive-half-line mass need not be the natural density.

This does not invalidate the limit, because the bad event has probability
\(o(1)\). It should nevertheless be repaired before circulation. The cleanest
repair is to define \(\nu_{g,t,\lambda}\) to be the genuine endpoint empirical
limit for every \(t\). Such a limit does exist even without full LI: the finite
trigonometric polynomial in (2.7) is equidistributed on the closure of the
cyclic subgroup generated by the angle vector (with the parity coordinate
included), and the \(o(1)\) remainder does not change the limit. On the LI set,
this general orbit-Haar law specializes to (2.12). The theorem can then retain
the words “actual endpoint law” and “natural density” without a convention.

The weaker alternative is to keep \(\delta_0\), but then the theorem and abstract
must explicitly call \(\nu_{g,t,\lambda}\) a *modified* law and must not identify
its value on the exceptional set with the actual race density.

### R2. The arithmetic-to-Haar replacement costs \(2\Pr(\mathrm{bad})\), not
literally \(\Pr(\mathrm{bad})\), for the displayed BL normalization

**Location:** `sections/05-assembly.tex`, first paragraph.

For the bounded--Lipschitz metric defined using
\(\|H\|_\infty\leq1\), coupling two random variables to agree off an event \(B\)
gives

\[
  |\mathbf EH(X)-\mathbf EH(Y)|\leq 2\Pr(B),
\]

not \(\Pr(B)\) unless the test functions are normalized to an interval of length
one. The factor is asymptotically irrelevant, but the sentence “changes the
distribution by at most the first probability” is false with the paper's
definition. Replace it by “at most twice the first probability” or simply
“by \(o(1)\).”

### R3. The Katz--Sarnak application should state the Frobenius convention and
integral-form input even more explicitly

**Location:** `sections/03-transfer.tex`, especially (4.1)--(4.5).

The half-Tate twist itself is valid. Because \(Q=s^2\) and \(3\nmid s\), a
constant continuous \(3\)-adic character can be chosen so that the chosen
Frobenius acts by \(s^{-1}\). It is geometrically trivial, changes weight one
to weight zero, and cancels the cup-product multiplier \(Q\). The residual
kernel cover has degree at most
\(|\operatorname{GL}(2g,\mathbf F_3)|<3^{4g^2}\).

However, this is a source-sensitive step and a referee will reasonably demand
the following in the proposition itself or an immediately preceding lemma:

1. say whether \(\operatorname{Fr}_Q\) denotes geometric or arithmetic
   Frobenius, and record the image of that element in
   \(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf F}_Q/\mathbf F_Q)\simeq\widehat{\mathbf Z}\);
2. specify the stable \(\mathbf Z_3\)-lattice used before reducing modulo \(3\);
3. state that the kernel cover trivializes the reduction of the *twisted*
   integral local system, not merely the untwisted cohomology;
4. explain in one sentence why tameness of the local system implies tameness of
   this residual finite cover at every boundary point;
5. state that every compact irreducible of \(\operatorname{USp}(2g)\) comes from
   the corresponding algebraic highest-weight representation over a finite
   extension of \(\mathbf Q_3\), which is the representation class covered by
   Katz--Sarnak 9.2.6.

The present text contains most of this information, but it is distributed
across sections and leaves the integral-form point implicit.

### R4. There is a much shorter and stronger Katz--Sarnak constant available

**Location:** `sections/03-transfer.tex`, proof of Proposition 4.1.

The level-cover proof appears valid, but it is unnecessarily expensive and
creates the most delicate source-dependent part of the transfer. Katz--Sarnak
9.2.5 gives, for a tame sheaf on an affine curve, the direct constant

\[
  C(X,\mathcal F)=2g_{\mathrm{base}}-2+N+\sum r_i.
\]

Here the compactified base is \(\mathbf P^1\), there are \(2g+1\) boundary
points (the \(2g\) deleted finite points and infinity), and every break is zero.
Thus one may take

\[
  C(U_g,\mathcal F_g)=2g-1.
\]

Katz--Sarnak 10.4.1 states this value for this exact family. The constant twist
is unramified and does not change the breaks. Replacing the mod-\(3\) cover
argument by this direct curve formula would strengthen (4.3), shorten the
paper, and remove a layer of avoidable arithmetic-geometry bookkeeping. The
headline growth regime remains sufficient because the heat trace, not the
factor \(3^{4g^2}\), already costs \(\exp(O(g^2\log g))\).

If the author keeps the cover proof as an independent self-contained bound,
the direct \(2g-1\) bound should at least be mentioned so readers do not infer
that the huge residual-cover constant is intrinsic.

### R5. The determinantal-process convergence citation is too compressed

**Location:** `sections/04-hard-edge.tex`, around (5.3)--(5.6).

The finite kernel and its local uniform limit are correct. For publication,
the sentence citing Soshnikov should identify an exact theorem and explicitly
say why its operator hypotheses hold. One clean route is:

- extend \(\widetilde K_g\) by zero beyond \(d/2\);
- on every compact interval, local uniform kernel convergence gives convergence
  of all determinant correlation functions;
- the uniform diagonal bound gives uniform local factorial-moment bounds and
  tightness;
- these imply vague convergence of the simple point processes.

At present, “local uniform convergence ... together with the diagonal bound” is
mathematically plausible and standard, but too terse for the central bridge of
the paper. The closed-edge no-escape estimate (5.6) is good and should remain.

### R6. The heat smoothing moment needs either a sharper reference or a short
standalone lemma

**Location:** `sections/A-heat-trace.tex`, (A.12).

The inequality

\[
  \int d(e,x)^2K_s(x)\,dm(x)\leq 2D_gs
\]

is consistent with the generator and metric conventions: the semigroup is
generated by the Laplace--Beltrami operator, the Euclidean coordinate variance
is \(2s\), and a compact group with a bi-invariant metric has nonnegative Ricci
curvature. The text's reference to “distributional squared-distance
comparison” is nevertheless a proof sketch, not a reference. Add a precise
lemma and cite a source for \(\Delta d(e,\cdot)^2\leq2D_g\) in the barrier or
distributional sense, or give the standard stopped-process argument plus smooth
approximation at the cut locus. Hall 1999 is not an obvious citation for this
specific distance-moment inequality.

### R7. The quotient-metric eigenangle matching assertion needs a citation or
proof

**Location:** `sections/03-transfer.tex`, end of Lemma 4.2.

The metric normalization on the maximal torus is correct, and the claimed
Weyl-matched Euclidean quotient distance is standard for compact Lie groups with
a bi-invariant metric. Since the global \(O_\lambda(g)\) Lipschitz constant is
one of the quantitative inputs to the theorem, cite the exact quotient-metric
result or prove it in a short lemma. Also state the harmless inequality
\(d_{\mathrm{conj}}([U],[V])\leq d_G(U,V)\) used to lift the class-space bound to
the whole group.

### R8. The Bessel concentration proof is sound in outline but should expose
the constants and inversion hypothesis

**Location:** `sections/04-hard-edge.tex`, (5.16)--(5.18).

For \(N\geq3\), the characteristic function
\(\prod_{j=1}^N J_0(a_jt)\) is uniformly integrable when
\(a_j\in[a_0,4]\), and the three-region argument does yield
\(O(N^{-1/2})\). To make this referee-proof, state explicit constants
\(\delta,T,c,r<1\) and write the three integrals. In the large-\(t\) region,
one should show

\[
 \int_T^\infty \left(C(a_0t)^{-1/2}\right)^Ndt
 \leq \frac{T}{N/2-1}\left(\frac{C}{\sqrt{a_0T}}\right)^N,
\]

after choosing \(T\) so the parenthesis is \(<1\). Then explicitly invoke
Fourier inversion from \(L^1\) of the characteristic function. The current
paragraph is correct but too compressed for a result whose sole purpose is to
separate scalar nondegeneracy from random-measure nondegeneracy.

### R9. The novelty claim is cautious, but the manuscript itself does not
contain a publication-grade current literature comparison

**Location:** introduction, “Relation to earlier work.”

The manuscript avoids the dangerous words “first,” “optimal,” and “solves
Katz--Sarnak,” which is good. Still, “Our contribution is ...” is a novelty
claim, and the bibliography is too small to certify it through August 2026.
The separate project novelty audit is useful internal work, but a submitted
paper needs a concise in-manuscript comparison to the closest results and a
fresh search immediately before posting. This proof audit cannot certify that
no overlapping theorem appeared after the cited preprints.

## 3. Reconstructed proof ledger

### 3.1 Exact race law: pass

Let \(r=Q^M>1\). From

\[
 \Psi(k)=\sum_{\deg F=k}\Lambda(F)\chi(F)
        =-\sum_{j=1}^{2g}\alpha_j^k
\]

and the separation of prime first powers,

\[
 -A(N)=\sum_{k\leq N}\sum_j(\alpha_jQ^m)^k+R_2(N)+R_{\geq3}(N).
\]

The sign of \(R_2\) is therefore positive in the inert-minus-split
normalization. For one conjugate pair, multiplication by
\(2(1-r^{-1})r^{-N}\) gives exactly

\[
 4(1-r^{-1})\Re\frac{e^{iN\vartheta}-r^{-N}}
                         {1-r^{-1}e^{-i\vartheta}},
\]

which yields (2.6) after the decaying term is removed.

For squares, the weighted unramified count is

\[
 \sum_{\deg P=\ell}\ell\chi(P)^2
   =Q^\ell+O(\ell Q^{\ell/2}+d\mathbf1_{\ell=1}).
\]

The main term is \(\sum_{\ell\leq N/2}r^{2\ell}\). Normalization gives
\(2r/(r+1)\) for \(N\) even and \(2/(r+1)\) for \(N\) odd. The exponent in the
error is
\((2M-1/2)\ell-MN\), whose maximum over \(\ell\leq N/2\) is at most
\(-\min(M,1/4)N+O(1)\).

For \(a\geq3\), the base-\(Q\) exponent is

\[
 E(a,\ell)=\ell+am\ell-MN,\qquad a\ell\leq N.
\]

- If \(m\geq0\), use \(a\ell\leq N\) and \(\ell\leq N/3\) to get
  \(E\leq-N/6\).
- If \(m<0\) and \(1+3m\geq0\), the maximum occurs at \(a=3\),
  \(\ell=N/3\), again giving \(-N/6\).
- If \(1+3m<0\), the maximum is \(-MN+O_M(1)\).

Thus all higher powers vanish for every \(M>0\). The parity-aware
Kronecker--Weyl step is also correct under
\(1,\vartheta_1/\pi,\ldots,\vartheta_g/\pi\) rationally independent.

### 3.2 Maximal Galois group to LI: pass

For \(Q=s^2\), the normalized roots \(\beta_j=\alpha_j/s\) are roots of
\(s^{-2g}R(sX)\in\mathbf Q[X]\), with the same splitting field and reciprocal
pair products equal to one. Kowalski's Proposition 2.4(2), for \(m=1\) and
\(g\geq2\), states that the rational multiplicative-relation space is precisely
the vectors whose two coordinates in every reciprocal pair are equal.

If
\(\sum a_j\vartheta_j+b\pi=0\), clearing denominators and exponentiating twice
produces the relation vector \((2a_j,0)\) on the \(j\)-th pair. Equality of pair
coordinates forces every \(a_j=0\), and the original relation then forces
\(b=0\). No unproved saturation assertion is used.

The original Kowalski Theorem 6.2 gives the exponent
\(\gamma_g=(4g^2+3g+5)^{-1}\) with an absolute implied constant for this exact
pencil. Erratum item 3 inserts the factor \(g^2\). Therefore (3.4) is the
corrected form. Under
\(\log Q_g/(g^2\log(g+2))\to\infty\), its logarithm tends to \(-\infty\), as
claimed.

### 3.3 Character transfer and heat bridge: pass, subject to R3--R7

The base Betti numbers are

\[
 h_c^0=0,\qquad h_c^1=2g,\qquad h_c^2=1,
\]

so Katz--Sarnak's \(A(U_g)=\sum_{i<2}h_c^i\) is \(2g\), not \(2g-1\), and the
field-size threshold is \(Q>16g^2\).

The same-phase coupling gives

\[
 W_2^2(T(\vartheta),T(\varphi))
 \leq \frac12\sum_j|b(\vartheta_j)-b(\varphi_j)|^2.
\]

Writing \(a=1-e^{-\lambda/d}\), \(r=e^{-\lambda/d}\), and
\(u=2\sqrt r\sin(\vartheta/2)/a\), differentiation gives

\[
 |b'(\vartheta)|\leq \frac{2\sqrt r}{a}
 \leq \frac{2(d+\lambda)}{\lambda},
\]

hence \(\operatorname{Lip}(T)=O_\lambda(g)\).

For the group metric \(-\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}(XY)\), the torus coordinates
are Euclidean, the type-\(C_g\) half-sum is
\(\rho=(g,g-1,\ldots,1)\), and

\[
 \kappa_\gamma=\sum_i\gamma_i(\gamma_i+2(g-i+1)).
\]

The \(g=1\) defining representation check gives Casimir \(3\), so there is no
hidden factor two. Peter--Weyl and Cauchy--Schwarz yield exactly

\[
 \varepsilon\left(\sum_\rho d_\rho^2e^{-2s\kappa_\rho}\right)^{1/2}
 =\varepsilon K_{2s}(e)^{1/2}.
\]

The crude Weyl-dimension shell bound is sufficient for

\[
 K_{2s}(e)\leq
 \exp(O(g^2\log(g+2)))\,s^{-(2g^2+g+1)/2}.
\]

With \(s=g^{-8}\), \(D_g=2g^2+g\), and \(L=O_\lambda(g)\), the smoothing cost
is \(O_\lambda(g^{-2})\), while the character term is
\(Q^{-1/2}\exp(O(g^2\log(g+2)))\). The stated simultaneous growth condition
therefore makes the transfer error vanish.

### 3.4 Haar hard edge and marked \(W_2\) limit: pass

The positive USp eigenangles form a projection DPP with kernel
\((2/\pi)\sum_{k=1}^g\sin(k\vartheta)\sin(k\varphi)\) relative to angle
Lebesgue measure. Under \(y=d\vartheta/(2\pi)\), the Jacobian produces

\[
 \widetilde K_g(x,y)=\frac4d\sum_{k=1}^g
 \sin\frac{2\pi kx}{d}\sin\frac{2\pi ky}{d}.
\]

The Riemann-sum limit is

\[
 \frac2\pi\int_0^\pi\sin(tx)\sin(ty)\,dt
 =\frac{\sin\pi(x-y)}{\pi(x-y)}-
   \frac{\sin\pi(x+y)}{\pi(x+y)}.
\]

The coefficient identity

\[
 a_{g,\lambda}(y)=
 \frac{4\sinh(\lambda/(2d))}
 {\sqrt{\sinh^2(\lambda/(2d))+\sin^2(\pi y/d)}}
\]

is exact. On compact sets it tends to
\(4\lambda/\sqrt{\lambda^2+(2\pi y)^2}\). Since
\(\sin(\pi y/d)\geq2y/d\),

\[
 a_{g,\lambda}(y)\leq
 \min\{4,\lambda e^{\lambda/10}/y\},
\]

and the intensity bound \(K_g(y,y)<2\) gives a uniform \(O(1/R)\) squared-tail
bound. The limiting integral is correctly normalized:

\[
 \int_0^\infty a_\lambda(y)^2\,dy=4\lambda.
\]

Local point matching on the *closed* half-line plus the two square-tail bounds
gives coefficient-vector convergence in \(\ell^2\). The shared-parity,
shared-phase coupling then gives convergence in \(W_2\), not just weak
convergence of the conditional laws.

### 3.5 Both nondegeneracy claims: pass

For the random conditional measure, the conditional variance is the DPP linear
statistic

\[
 V_\lambda=\sum_{y\in\Pi_{\mathrm{Sp}}}
 \frac{8\lambda^2}{\lambda^2+4\pi^2y^2}.
\]

Because the symplectic kernel is a projection,

\[
 \operatorname{Var}V_\lambda
 =\frac12\iint(f_\lambda(x)-f_\lambda(y))^2|K(x,y)|^2\,dx\,dy>0.
\]

The \(1/2\) factor, integrability, and positivity are all correct. This proves
that the full random probability measure is nonconstant, but correctly is not
used as a proof for the scalar half-line mass.

For the scalar, symmetry and absolute continuity give

\[
 F_\lambda(\xi)=\frac12\bigl(1+\Pr_\Phi(|S_\xi|<1)\bigr)>\frac12.
\]

The DPP has positive probability of at least \(N\) points in any fixed interval
near (but not containing) zero: its \(N\)-th factorial moment is positive because
the sine functions associated with distinct points are linearly independent.
On that event, the selected coefficients lie in a compact subinterval of
\((2,4]\), and the uniform Bessel estimate makes the small-ball probability
\(O(N^{-1/2})\). Hence values of \(F_\lambda\) occur arbitrarily close to
\(1/2\) on positive-probability events, contradicting constancy at any
\(c>1/2\).

   end-to-end Lean verified.

## 7. Honest quality assessment from this audit

As mathematics, the current manuscript is a credible specialist bridge theorem
with a coherent proof and an interesting quenched \(\mathcal P_2\)-valued
endpoint. It is not presently a “9.5/10, bullet-proof” paper: the result uses
very rapid field growth, its deepest arithmetic inputs are imported, the
novelty comparison is not yet publication-grade, and several central standard
arguments are compressed past the level a skeptical referee will accept. None
of those observations means the theorem is false. They mean the honest next
phase is source-tightening, external review, and artifact verification rather
than adding more theorems.
