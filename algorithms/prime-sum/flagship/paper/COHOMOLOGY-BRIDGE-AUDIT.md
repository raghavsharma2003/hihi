## 1. Frobenius and the half-Tate character

### 1.1 Convention: PASS

Kowalski defines local geometric Frobenius as the inverse of the canonical
arithmetic generator and records that its degree in the arithmetic quotient
is `-n` at a point of degree `n`.  He also writes

```text
P_C(T) = det(1 - T Fr_geom | H^1(C_bar,Q_l))
```

and states that this same geometric Frobenius is a symplectic similitude of
multiplier `q` for the cup-product pairing into `Q_l(-1)`.  Therefore, for
`Q=s^2`, the normalization needed here is exactly

```text
eta_s(Fr_arith,Q) = s,
eta_s(Fr_geom,Q)  = s^{-1}.
```

This is precisely (4.2).  It gives normalized eigenvalues `alpha_j/s` and
changes the multiplier from `Q` to `Q s^{-2}=1`.  There is no remaining
arithmetic/geometric inversion error.

Primary corroboration: E. Kowalski, *The large sieve, monodromy and zeta
functions of curves*, Section 2 and equations (6.1)--(6.2),
<https://people.math.ethz.ch/~kowalski/large-sieve-monodromy.pdf>.

### 1.2 Existence and continuity: PASS

The exact sequence for the connected base has quotient

```text
pi_1(U_g) / pi_1(U_g over Fbar_Q) = Gal(Fbar_Q/F_Q) = Z-hat.
```

The theorem assumes `g>=2` and `char(F_Q)>2g+1`, so the characteristic is not
`3`; hence `s` is a unit in `Z_3`.  The closure of the cyclic subgroup
generated by `s` in `Z_3^x` is a profinite procyclic group.  The homomorphism
`n -> s^n` from `Z` therefore extends continuously from `Z-hat` to that
closure.  Pullback along the arithmetic quotient gives the required constant
rank-one `Q_3` local system.  At a degree-`n` point its geometric-Frobenius
eigenvalue is `s^{-n}=Q^{-n/2}`.  Thus `eta_s` is pure of weight `-1`, while
`R^1 pi_* Q_3` has weight `1`, and their tensor product has weight `0`.

Equivalently, `eta_s^2` is the constant Tate character `Q_3(1)` on this
base.  Tensoring the original pairing

```text
R^1 pi_* Q_3 x R^1 pi_* Q_3 -> Q_3(-1)
```

by `eta_s^2` makes its target the trivial sheaf.  This verifies the claim
that every arithmetic fundamental-group element, not merely rational-point
Frobenius elements, acts symplectically after the twist.

### 1.3 Arithmetic monodromy: PASS, CONDITIONAL ON THE GEOMETRIC IMPORT

The constant character is trivial on the geometric fundamental group, so the
geometric monodromy of the twist is unchanged.  Once the cited geometric
monodromy theorem gives Zariski closure `Sp(2g)`, the twisted arithmetic image
is contained in `Sp(2g)` by the preceding pairing calculation and contains a
Zariski-dense geometric subgroup.  Hence its arithmetic Zariski closure is
also exactly `Sp(2g)`.  The normalized semisimple Frobenius classes have unit
complex eigenvalues in reciprocal pairs and consequently determine the
claimed compact-form classes in `USp(2g)`.

## 3. Algebraic representations and purity

### 3.1 Compact irreducibles and 3-adic models: PASS

Irreducible representations of `USp(2g)` are indexed by dominant integral
type-`C_g` highest weights and are restrictions of the corresponding
irreducible algebraic representations of `Sp(2g)_C`.  Since split `Sp(2g)`
has a Chevalley model over `Z`, the characteristic-zero highest-weight module
can be formed over `Q` and then base-changed to `Q_3`.  Thus the phrase
"after choosing a 3-adic model" is legitimate.  An integral `Z_3` lattice is
not needed: the direct GOS argument uses a `Q_3` local system and never reduces
it modulo `3`.

Applying such an algebraic representation to the monodromy representation
gives a lisse local system of rank `dim(rho)`.  Wild inertia was already
trivial for `F_g`, and an algebraic representation sends the identity to the
identity, so `rho(F_g)` remains tame at every boundary point.

### 3.2 Purity: PASS

At every closed point, the semisimple eigenvalues of `F_g` have complex
absolute value one and lie in a symplectic torus.  The eigenvalues after
applying `rho` are evaluations of the integral weights of `rho`, hence
Laurent monomials in those torus eigenvalues.  They also have absolute value
one.  Therefore `rho(F_g)` is punctually pure of weight zero.  This supplies
the exact hypothesis needed for the weight bound on compactly supported
cohomology; it is not merely an assertion that the standard representation
is pure.

The trace of geometric Frobenius on `rho(F_g)_t` is the same integral
character polynomial as `chi_rho(Theta_{g,t})`, so the cohomological trace
sum is exactly the character sum in Proposition 4.1.

## 4. End cohomology and the exact GOS computation

Let `r=dim(rho)` and work over the algebraic closure of `F_Q`.

### 4.1 `H_c^0=0`: PASS

The relevant reason is that `U_g` is geometrically connected and nonproper,
not merely the word "affine" in isolation.  A compactly supported section of
a lisse sheaf is locally constant; if nonzero on a connected component, its
support is both open and closed and hence is all of `U_g`, which is not
proper.  Thus it must vanish.  The manuscript's conclusion is right, though
"because the base is affine" is compressed wording.

### 4.2 `H_c^2=0`: PASS

Poincare duality on a smooth curve identifies the dual of `H_c^2(U,F)` with
`H^0(U,F^vee(1))`.  Tate twisting does not change the geometric invariant
space.  The geometric fundamental group is Zariski dense in `Sp(2g)`, and the
dual of a nontrivial irreducible algebraic representation has no `Sp(2g)`
invariants.  Therefore `H_c^2=0`.

For a standard accessible statement of the duality pairing, see J. S. Milne,
*Lectures on Etale Cohomology*, Theorem 14.7,
<https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/LEC.pdf>.

### 4.3 GOS and all boundary terms: PASS

The compactification is `P^1`, and the boundary consists of the `2g` deleted
finite roots together with infinity.  Consequently

```text
chi_c(U_g,Q_3) = 2-(2g+1) = 1-2g.
```

For a lisse sheaf on an open smooth curve, the compact-support
Grothendieck--Ogg--Shafarevich formula is

```text
chi_c(U,F) = rank(F) chi_c(U,Q_l) - sum_x Swan_x(F).
```

There is **no additional tame inertia-drop term in this compact-support
formula**.  Such rank-minus-invariant terms occur in conductor formulas for
extensions/middle extensions and are a common source of confusion here.
Katz--Sarnak tameness, preserved by the constant twist and by `rho`, makes
every Swan conductor zero, including the one at infinity.  Hence

```text
chi_c(U_g,rho(F_g)) = (1-2g) r.
```

With `H_c^0=H_c^2=0`, this gives the exact identity

```text
dim H_c^1(U_g_bar,rho(F_g)) = (2g-1) dim(rho).
```

No boundary term, factor of two, or additive rank is missing.

## 5. Trace formula, weights, and constants

### 5.1 Trace formula and Deligne exponent: PASS

The Grothendieck--Lefschetz formula with the manuscript's geometric
Frobenius convention gives

```text
sum_{t in U_g(F_Q)} chi_rho(Theta_{g,t})
  = sum_i (-1)^i Tr(Fr_geom,Q | H_c^i).
```

Only degree one remains.  A sheaf pure of weight zero has `H_c^1` mixed of
weights at most one, so every eigenvalue has complex absolute value at most
`Q^(1/2)`.  Therefore

```text
|sum_t chi_rho(Theta_{g,t})|
 <= (2g-1) dim(rho) sqrt(Q).
```

This is the exact power of `Q`; neither `Q` nor a missing normalized factor
appears.

Kowalski's primary paper states the same trace-formula and weight mechanism
in its Section 5, and Deligne is correctly cited as the underlying source.

### 5.2 Denominator and second inequality: PASS

All `2g` removed points are distinct and `F_Q`-rational, so

```text
|U_g(F_Q)| = Q-2g.
```

Dividing gives the first inequality in (4.3).  If `Q>4g`, then
`Q-2g>Q/2`, hence

```text
(2g-1)sqrt(Q)/(Q-2g) < 2(2g-1)/sqrt(Q).
```

Thus both the hypothesis and numerical factor in Proposition 4.1 are right.

## 6. Conjugacy quotient and Lipschitz metric

### 6.1 Torus normalization: PASS

For

```text
X(x)=diag(ix_1,...,ix_g,-ix_1,...,-ix_g),
```

direct multiplication gives

```text
-1/2 Tr(X(x)X(y)) = sum_i x_i y_i.
```

Therefore angle displacement has exactly the Euclidean norm used in Lemma
4.2; there is no factor `sqrt(2)`.

### 6.2 Affine-Weyl minimization: PASS WITH WORDING WEAKNESS

The quotient distance can be made completely explicit as

```text
min_{sigma in S_g, epsilon_j in {+1,-1}, k in Z^g}
 || theta - (epsilon_j phi_{sigma(j)} + 2 pi k_j)_j ||_2.
```

For `theta,phi in [0,pi]`, changing the sign of a matched angle gives the
alternative coordinate distances `theta+phi` or `2pi-theta-phi`.  Both are
at least `|theta-phi|`.  Thus signs and `2pi` translations cannot improve the
ordinary nonnegative-angle match.  Among permutations, the rearrangement
inequality makes increasing-to-increasing matching minimal.  This proves

```text
d_conj([U],[V])^2 = sum_j |theta_j-phi_j|^2.
```

The inequality `d_conj<=d_G(U,V)` is immediate because the orbit-distance
infimum includes the original representatives.  Hence the metric input to
the Wasserstein coupling is correct.  The current prose invokes the standard
compact-group orbit result but does not display the `2pi` translations; the
argument above would be a useful publication-level clarification, not a
repair of a false formula.

### 6.3 Same-phase factor: PASS

Under the shared parity/shared-phase coupling, centers cancel and cross terms
vanish.  Since `E cos^2(Phi_j)=1/2`, the coupling cost is exactly one half of
the squared coefficient difference.  Combining it with the Euclidean
quotient metric gives `Lip(T)<=||b'||_infty/sqrt(2)`.  The derivative bound
then gives (4.7) with the displayed `sqrt(2)`; the factors are consistent.

## 7. Brownian displacement and heat normalization

### 7.1 Generator and variance: PASS

For an orthonormal left-invariant frame on this compact unimodular group,
the Laplace--Beltrami operator is

```text
Delta = sum_j X_j^2.
```

Thus `A=-sum X_j^2` and `P_s=e^{-sA}=e^{s Delta}`.  Its Euclidean local model
has coordinate variance `2s`, exactly as stated.  The `g=1` Casimir check is
also correct: with metric `-Tr(XY)/2`, the basis `i sigma_1,i sigma_2,i
sigma_3` is orthonormal and the defining representation has eigenvalue `3`.

### 7.2 Distance moment: PASS

On a `D`-dimensional complete manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature,
Laplacian comparison gives `Delta r <=(D-1)/r` away from the base point and
cut locus, with the same upper bound in the barrier/distributional sense.
The singular cut-locus contribution has the favorable (nonpositive) sign.
Consequently

```text
Delta r^2 <= 2D
```

distributionally.  Applying the positive heat semigroup and integrating from
zero to `s` yields

```text
E_x d(x,B_s)^2 = P_s(r^2)(x) <= 2Ds.
```

For a bi-invariant metric,
`Ric(X,X)=1/4 sum_j ||[X,X_j]||^2>=0`, so the lemma applies to `USp(2g)`.
The sign and the constant `2D` are correct for the manuscript's generator.

The sentence about smooth bounded approximations is a legitimate way to
justify the distributional semigroup step on a compact manifold.  No hidden
stopping-time or injectivity-radius error was found.

### 7.3 Peter--Weyl and `K_{2s}`: PASS

With Haar probability, irreducible characters are orthonormal in the central
subspace, while all `d_rho^2` normalized matrix coefficients form the full
Peter--Weyl basis.  Therefore

```text
K_t(e) = sum_rho d_rho^2 exp(-t kappa_rho).
```

Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the smoothed character expansion produces
`exp(-2s kappa_rho)` after squaring.  The exact term is consequently
`K_{2s}(e)^(1/2)`, not `K_s(e)^(1/2)`.  Because the density is relative to
Haar probability, no Riemannian volume factor occurs.  The two smoothing
errors, one under each probability measure, give the factor
`2L sqrt(2D_gs)` in (A.10).

## 8. Feeding the direct character bound into the heat bridge

Set

```text
epsilon_g = 2(2g-1)/sqrt(Q),
D_g       = 2g^2+g,
s         = g^{-8},
L_g       = O_lambda(g).
```

Then

```text
2 L_g sqrt(2D_g s) = O_lambda(g * g * g^{-4})
                    = O_lambda(g^{-2}).
```

The heat-trace lemma has `N=2g^2+g` and gives

```text
K_{2s}(e)^(1/2)
 <= exp(O(g^2 log(g+2))) s^{-(N+1)/4}.
```

At `s=g^{-8}`, the last power is itself
`exp(O(g^2 log(g+2)))`.  Multiplication by
`epsilon_g=O(g Q^{-1/2})` only changes the absolute constant in that
exponent.  Hence the second term in (4.8) is exactly of the claimed form

```text
Q^{-1/2} exp(C g^2 log(g+2)).
```

Finally,
`log Q/(g^2 log(g+2))->infinity` forces its logarithm to tend to minus
infinity.  The passage from the character estimate to the bounded--Lipschitz
transfer therefore closes with no lost genus or field factor.

## 9. Release assessment for this bridge

The current direct cohomological proof is mathematically stronger, shorter,
and less source-sensitive than the removed residual-cover proof.  In
particular, it does **not** require:

- a stable `Z_3` lattice for every `rho`;
- reduction of `rho(F_g)` modulo `3`;
- a residual trivializing cover;
- a genus-exponential cover-degree or Betti-number bound.

The bridge may be called internally verified **conditional on the cited
Katz--Sarnak monodromy and tameness theorem**.  It should not be advertised as
fully first-principles or formally verified: the deep monodromy theorem,
tameness theorem, GOS, Poincare duality, trace formula, and Weil-II weight
theorem remain external mathematical imports, and the exact Katz--Sarnak
book pages were not independently inspected in this audit.

Two optional exposition improvements remain:

1. replace "`H_c^0` vanishes because the base is affine" by the precise
   connected-nonproper/lisse-section explanation;
2. display the affine-Weyl `2pi`-translation minimization in the conjugacy
   quotient lemma.

Neither is a correction to a false estimate.
