## The post

---

I asked two rival AI models to tear my prime-number paper apart. The first
one gave it 6.8 out of 10.

Best thing that's happened to this project.

Here's what they actually found: a paragraph contradicting another
paragraph. A table of numbers my own later section said were wrong. The
word "certified" used for something that wasn't certified. Small stuff. The
kind of stuff a reviewer finds in five minutes and then stops trusting you.

So we fixed all of it — and then went after the two hardest things on their
list, the ones marked "very high value, probably too difficult."

Both worked. The paper went from 15 pages to 59, and it now has four
theorems instead of one:

1. The bias slides, it doesn't jump. Give primes more weight and the famous
99.6% bias falls — smoothly, with no gaps or surprises, the whole way down.

2. It dissolves at a knowable speed. That was a pattern we'd measured. Now
it's proved, with an error bar.

3. Two different fades are one fade. Mathematicians already knew the bias
weakens if you change the number system you're working in. We showed their
version and our weight version are the same theorem wearing different
clothes.

4. It never comes back. Past a certain weight the bias only ever decreases
— it can't wobble back up. (Proved beyond that point; below it, strong
evidence and an honest conjecture.)

Plus: a computer *proved* our key constant correct to 30 decimal places.
Not calculated — proved, in a system where every number carries a
guaranteed margin of error.

What I want to be honest about: all of this stands on the Riemann
Hypothesis being true, which nobody has proved. That's standard in this
corner of maths — every paper here makes the same bet — but it's a bet, and
the paper says so on page one. Four things remain genuinely open, and
they're listed too.

The lesson I'll keep: I got further by inviting attacks than by defending
anything. Every criticism that survived checking made the work better.
Every one that didn't, I could show was wrong — and that's worth just as
much.

Same links in the comments. Still a learner. Still want experts to find
things.
