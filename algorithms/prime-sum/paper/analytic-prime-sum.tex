\begin{abstract}
We describe a single algorithmic framework that computes weighted prime sums
$S_m(x)=\sum_{p\le x}p^{\,m}$ from nontrivial zeros of $\zeta(s)$, uniformly
in the weight $m$, and its extension to arithmetic progressions using zeros
of Dirichlet $L$-functions. A floating-point implementation recovers, from
only the first $2{,}000$ zeta zeros: the exact integers $S_1(x)$ up to
$x=10^9$ (e.g.\ $S_1(10^9)=24{,}739{,}512{,}092{,}254{,}535$), exact
$\pi(x)=S_0(x)$ up to $10^8$, exact $S_2(x)$ and $S_3(x)$ up to $10^7$, and
$S_{-1}(x)=\sum_{p\le x}1/p$ to $17$ significant digits; and, from $511$
zeros of $L(s,\chi_4)$ computed as part of the pipeline, the exact sums of
primes in both progressions mod $4$ up to $10^7$. The framework combines a
logarithmic-Gaussian smoothing whose Mellin transform is exactly Gaussian
with an integral representation
$1/\log n=\int_0^A n^{-\sigma}d\sigma+n^{-A}/\log n$ that removes the
logarithmic weight; the resulting integrands are elementary ($\erfc$ and
exponentials only), every pole collision resolves by one cancellation
pattern, and no logarithmic-integral functions or branch tracking appear
anywhere. The formulation is equivalent, after an interchange of integrals,
to the classical $\log\zeta$ representation of Lagarias--Odlyzko and Platt;
its value is operational and its uniformity in $m$ and in the $L$-function
aspect. To our knowledge, no implementation recovering exact prime sums ---
in any weight, or in progressions --- has been published. We report measured
error phenomena of independent practical interest, including a coherent
double-precision rounding bias that oscillates at zeta-zero frequencies, and
a zero-ordinate precision floor scaling like $x^{m+1/2}\delta_\gamma\log x$.
All bounds in this draft are heuristic and numerically validated; the path
to a rigorous interval-arithmetic version is laid out.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Computing $\pi(x)$ without enumerating primes has two classical lines. The
combinatorial line (Legendre, Meissel, Lehmer,
Lagarias--Miller--Odlyzko, Del\'eglise--Rivat~\cite{DR96}, Staple's
space-efficient variant~\cite{Staple15}, Walisch's \texttt{primecount})
holds the records unconditionally. The analytic line evaluates $\pi(x)$ from
zeros of $\zeta(s)$: proposed by Lagarias--Odlyzko~\cite{LO87}, first
seriously engineered in Galway's thesis~\cite{Galway04}, made rigorous and
unconditional by Platt~\cite{Platt15} ($\pi(10^{24})$), and made practical
at record scale by Franke--Kleinjung--B\"uthe--Jost~\cite{FKBJ17}
($\pi(10^{24})$ under RH) and B\"uthe~\cite{Buthe14} ($\pi(10^{25})$), the
latter two working on the Weil--Barner ($\zeta'/\zeta$) side of the explicit
formula. Numerical evaluation of the classical Riemann decomposition with
$\li(x^{\rho})$ terms goes back to Riesel--G\"ohl~\cite{RG70}.

For the \emph{sum} of primes $S_1(x)=\sum_{p\le x}p$, the combinatorial
theory transfers (Lucy\_Hedgehog's $O(x^{3/4})$ method~\cite{Lucy13},
Fenwick-tree variants~\cite{Broxey23}, Walisch's
\texttt{primesum}~\cite{Walisch} with records past $10^{20}$). The analytic
line, however, appears never to have been \emph{carried out} for prime sums:
the underlying explicit formula --- the first Riesz mean
$\sum_{n\le x}(x-n)\Lambda(n)$ and its siblings --- is textbook material
\cite{MV}, and explicit asymptotic bounds for $S_1(x)$ exist
\cite{Axler14}, but we are aware of no published implementation recovering
exact values of $S_1(x)$, of $S_m(x)$ for any other weight, or of prime
sums in arithmetic progressions. (A recent preprint~\cite{TG25} claims exact
$\pi(x)$ from a truncated-Gaussian kernel with remarkably few zeros and no
sieve correction; its error analysis appears to us incompatible with the
uncertainty principle constraints discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:complexity}, and our method is unrelated.) This paper
fills the computational gap, at working-algorithm level, uniformly in the
weight and in the $L$-function aspect.

\subsection*{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{First implementation of analytic prime sums.} An algorithm
    computing $S_m(x)$ from zeta zeros with exact integer recovery,
    validated for $m=1$ to $x=10^9$, $m=0$ (i.e.\ $\pi(x)$) to $10^8$,
    $m\in\{2,3\}$ to $10^7$, and $m=-1$ (Mertens sums, non-integer target)
    to $17$ digits (Table~\ref{tab:results}). The underlying identities are
    classical; the contribution is the uniform algorithm, its error
    budget, and the verified implementation.
  \item \textbf{An operationally simple formulation.} All computations run
    through one device: a log-Gaussian cutoff with exactly Gaussian Mellin
    transform, and the representation
    $1/\log n=\int_0^A n^{-\sigma}d\sigma+n^{-A}/\log n$ integrated against
    a family of smoothed Chebyshev-type formulas
    (Section~\ref{sec:logstrip}). This is equivalent, after interchanging
    the $\sigma$-integral with the contour integral, to the classical
    $\log\zeta$ representation~\cite{LO87,Platt15}; what the
    reparametrization buys is that every integrand is elementary
    ($\erfc$, $\exp$, $\zeta'/\zeta$ on the real axis), every singularity
    is a removable pair-cancellation handled by one lemma, no
    $\li(x^{\rho})$, kernel special functions~\cite{FKBJ17,Buthe14}, or
    branch tracking~\cite{Platt15} occur, and the same code path serves
    every weight $m$ and every Dirichlet character.
  \item \textbf{Arithmetic progressions, exactly.} Using $511$ zeros of
    $L(s,\chi_4)$ computed within the pipeline (Hardy $Z$-scan with derived
    phase, Illinois refinement), the twisted formula --- which has no main
    term --- recovers the exact sums of primes in both progressions mod
    $4$ at $x=10^5,10^6,10^7$. Prior computations from Dirichlet zeros
    (Bays--Hudson~\cite{BH00}, at much larger $x$) were approximate
    studies of prime-race irregularities; exact integer recovery appears
    to be new. We present this as an engineering result: given
    Contribution 2, the extension is natural, and that naturalness is the
    point.
  \item \textbf{Measured numerical phenomena} that any implementation of
    windowed analytic prime computation will face
    (Section~\ref{sec:cautions}): a coherent double-precision rounding
    bias of size $\approx x^{m+1}2^{-52}$ oscillating at zeta-zero
    frequencies (perfectly impersonating a missing explicit-formula term),
    and a zero-ordinate precision floor $\sim x^{m+1/2}\delta_\gamma\log x$
    that already forces $40$-digit ordinates at $m=3$, $x=10^7$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Setup}\label{sec:setup}

$x\ge2$ is the integer target, $\eps>0$ the smoothing width, $L=\log x$,
$\rho=\beta+i\gamma$ runs over nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$ (RH holds in the
verified ranges used; formulas are stated with general $\beta$). The
logarithmic-Gaussian cutoff is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff}
  c(t)=\tfrac12\,\erfc\!\Bigl(\frac{\log(t/x)}{\sqrt2\,\eps}\Bigr).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Mellin transform]\label{lem:mellin}
For $\operatorname{Re}s>0$,
$M(s):=\int_0^\infty c(t)t^{s-1}dt=\dfrac{x^s}{s}e^{s^2\eps^2/2}$,
which continues meromorphically with a single simple pole at $s=0$ of
residue~$1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Smoothed Perron]\label{lem:perron}
For $c_0>0$ and $n\ge1$:
$\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(c_0)}M(s)n^{-s}ds=c(n)$.
\end{lemma}

Proofs are elementary (substitution $t=xe^u$, integration by parts,
Gaussian moment generating function; completing the square). Both are in
the source files accompanying this draft.

\section{The smoothed explicit formula, uniformly in the weight}
\label{sec:explicit}

For $m\in\mathbb Z$ and $\sigma\ge0$ set
$\widetilde\psi_m(\sigma)=\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)\,n^{m-\sigma}c(n)$.
Perron with $A(s)=-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s+\sigma-m)$ and
Lemma~\ref{lem:perron}, then a contour shift, give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit}
  \widetilde\psi_m(\sigma)=M(m+1-\sigma)-\sum_{\rho}M(\rho+m-\sigma)
  -\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(\sigma-m)-\sum_{k\ge1}M(m-2k-\sigma),
\end{equation}
with the zero sum absolutely convergent at Gaussian speed
$|M(\rho+m-\sigma)|\ll x^{\beta+m-\sigma}e^{-\gamma^2\eps^2/2}/|\gamma|$.
For the twisted case, $\chi$ a primitive odd character mod $q$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicitchi}
  \widetilde\psi_{1,\chi}(\sigma)=\sum_n\Lambda(n)\chi(n)n^{1-\sigma}c(n)
  =-\sum_{\rho_\chi}M(\rho_\chi+1-\sigma)-\frac{L'}{L}(\sigma-1,\chi)
  -\sum_{k\ge0}M(-2k-\sigma),
\end{equation}
with \emph{no main term} ($L$ entire).

\subsection*{Pole collisions and the one cancellation pattern}
Along the $\sigma$-integration of Section~\ref{sec:logstrip} the terms of
\eqref{eq:explicit}--\eqref{eq:explicitchi} individually blow up at
isolated real points, always in cancelling pairs, always resolved by the
expansion $M(w)=\frac1w+\log x+O(w)$ near $w=0$ against a logarithmic
derivative's pole:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
crossing & colliding pair & finite limit \\
\midrule
$\sigma=m+1$ (any $m$) & $M(m+1-\sigma)$ vs.\ pole of $\zeta$ at $1$ &
$\log x-\gamma_E+O(\eps^2)$ \\
$\sigma=0$, $m=-1$ & $M(-\sigma)$ vs.\ pole of $\zeta'/\zeta$ at $1$ &
$\log x-\gamma_E+O(\eps^2)$ \\
$\sigma=0$, $m=2$ & $M(-\sigma)$ (triv.\ $k=1$) vs.\ trivial zero at $-2$ &
$-\log x-\tfrac{\zeta''(-2)}{2\zeta'(-2)}+O(\eps^2)$ \\
$\sigma=0$, odd $\chi$ & $M(-\sigma)$ (triv.\ $k=0$) vs.\ trivial zero at
$-1$ & $-\log x-\tfrac{L''(-1)}{2L'(-1)}+O(\eps^2)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Each limit was verified numerically to the working precision before use
(linear residual in $\sigma$, correct constant), and each formula
\eqref{eq:explicit}/\eqref{eq:explicitchi} was validated \emph{pointwise}
against brute-force evaluation of its defining sum at several $\sigma$
before any integration --- a protocol we found indispensable
(Section~\ref{sec:cautions}).

\section{Removing the logarithmic weight}\label{sec:logstrip}

With $\widetilde T_m=\sum_{p^k}\frac{p^{km}}{k}c(p^k)
=\sum_n\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\log n}n^{m}c(n)$ and any $A>m+1$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logstrip}
  \widetilde T_m=\int_0^A\widetilde\psi_m(\sigma)\,d\sigma+R_A,\qquad
  R_A=\sum_{p^k}\frac1k\,p^{-(A-m)k}c(p^k),
\end{equation}
by $1/\log n=\int_0^A n^{-\sigma}d\sigma+n^{-A}/\log n$ under the
absolutely convergent sum. We use $A=m+3$, so $R_A$ converges like a prime
zeta series ($p^{-3k}$ weights; truncation at $p\le10^6$ costs
$<10^{-13}$). Term-by-term $\sigma$-integration of \eqref{eq:explicit} is
justified on $[0,A]$ by uniform Gaussian domination of the zero sum.

\begin{remark}[Equivalence with the $\log\zeta$ representation]
Since $\int_0^A-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s+\sigma-m)\,d\sigma
=\log\zeta(s-m)-\log\zeta(s+A-m)$, interchanging the $\sigma$-integral
with the Perron integral shows \eqref{eq:logstrip} is the classical
$\log\zeta$ formulation of~\cite{LO87,Platt15}, with $R_A$ playing the
role of the discarded $\log\zeta(s+A-m)$ piece and the pair cancellations
of Section~\ref{sec:explicit} the $\sigma$-side shadow of the integrable
logarithmic singularity of $\log\zeta$. We therefore claim no new analytic
mechanism --- only a parametrization in which every integrand is
elementary and real-axis, and which the computations of
Section~\ref{sec:results} suggest is a good one to compute with.
\end{remark}

\section{Assembly, error budget, and cost}\label{sec:algorithm}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:assembly}
  S_m(x)=\widetilde T_m
  -\!\!\sum_{k\ge2,\;p^k}\!\!\frac{p^{km}}k c(p^k)
  -\sum_{p}p^{m}\bigl(c(p)-[p\le x]\bigr)\;(+\,\text{error}),
\end{equation}
rounded to the nearest integer for $m\ge0$ (for $m=-1$ the output is a
high-precision value). The corrections are exact sieves: prime powers up
to $(xe^{K\eps})^{1/2}$ and a window $|\log(p/x)|\le K\eps$, $K=12$. For
progressions mod $4$: $\sum_{p\equiv a}p\,c(p)=\tfrac12[S_{\mathrm{odd}}
\pm S_\chi]$ with $S_\chi$ from \eqref{eq:explicitchi}, window-corrected
per progression.

Error budget (all lines measured to behave as predicted): zero tail
$O\!\bigl(x^{m+1/2}e^{-(\gamma_N\eps)^2/2}/(\gamma_N^2\eps^2)\bigr)$;
zero-ordinate noise $O(x^{m+1/2}L\,\delta_\gamma\sqrt N)$ --- the
\emph{precision floor} that forced $40$-digit ordinates at $m=3$,
$x=10^7$; quadrature/floating-point noise; truncation constants
($R_A$ tail, $K\eps$ cutoffs, trivial-zero tails) below $10^{-13}$.

\subsection{Cost}\label{sec:complexity}
Analytic part $O(N)$ node-evaluations (times precision polylogs); window
sieve $O(x\eps\log x)=O((x/\gamma_N)\,\mathrm{polylog})$. Balancing gives
$N\asymp x^{1/2}\,\mathrm{polylog}$ and total $O(x^{1/2+\eps})$ --- the
Lagarias--Odlyzko exponent~\cite{LO87}; the window is also the reason few
zeros suffice: the $\Theta(x\eps/\log x)$ sieved primes carry the
information the truncated zero sum cannot, which is why we regard
sieve-free exactness claims with very few zeros~\cite{TG25} as
incompatible with this tradeoff.

\section{Numerical cautions}\label{sec:cautions}

\subsection{Coherent rounding at zero frequencies}
Evaluating the window correction $\sum_p p^m(c(p)-[p\le x])$ in IEEE double
produced a discrepancy against the explicit formula that was independent of
$\eps$, grew like $x^{m+1}$, and \emph{oscillated in $\log x$ at the
frequencies of the first zeta zeros} --- the exact signature of a missing
zero term, which the contour analysis excludes. Cause: catastrophic
cancellation in $\log p-\log x$ for window primes makes deterministic
$\delta c\sim10^{-13}$ errors that are weighted by $p^m\approx x^m$ and
inherit the window population's zero-frequency fluctuations. Diagnosis: one
full recomputation in $40$-digit arithmetic collapsed the ``missing term''
from $-1.2\times10^{-6}$ to $-3.4\times10^{-10}$ (the true tail) at
$x=6\times10^4$. Remedy (cheap): $u=\operatorname{log1p}((p-x)/x)$ from
exact integers, $\erfc$ beyond double, throughout the window. We flag this
because the symptom is maximally misleading and any windowed analytic prime
computation faces it.

\subsection{Zero-ordinate precision floor; quadrature nodes}
Per-zero phase error $\delta_\gamma L$ enters at amplitude
$\approx2x^{m+1/2}e^{-\gamma^2\eps^2/2}/(\gamma L)$: at $m=3$, $x=10^7$,
$25$-digit ordinates miss the integer by $-16.9$; Newton-refining the
leading $550$ zeros to $40$ digits (two steps of $s-\zeta/\zeta'$, seconds)
restores $10^{-3}$ residuals. The same estimate reproduces observed
residuals at every $(m,x)$. Separately, Gauss--Legendre nodes taken from
double-precision tables inject $\sim1$ absolute error at $x^{5/2}$ scale
--- nodes must be computed at working precision. A validation protocol of
(i) pointwise formula-vs-brute checks before integration and (ii) an
independent brute check of $\widetilde T_m$ caught one genuine
implementation bug (a doubly-applied $1/|\rho|$ factor) that would have
scaled silently from $-0.12$ at $10^5$ to $\sim\times300$ at $10^7$.

\section{Toward rigor and records}\label{sec:rigor}

The pipeline resists rigor nowhere in principle; the components exist in
the $\pi(x)$ literature~\cite{Platt15,FKBJ17,Buthe14}: quantitative
contour lemmas, interval arithmetic with directed rounding, certified zero
enclosures (LMFDB, or computed as in~\cite{Platt15}), and explicit tail
bounds via Riemann--von Mangoldt counts with explicit constants. For
Mertens sums our floating-point $17$-digit agreement should become a
certified enclosure, the natural comparison being
B\"uthe's~\cite{Buthe15} zero-based control of Mertens-theorem error terms
(the closest published antecedent to our $m=-1$ instance) and
Brent--Platt--Trudgian~\cite{BPT20} for rigorous zero-sum evaluation.
Records ($S_1(x)$ beyond \texttt{primesum}'s combinatorial range, or exact
AP sums at Bays--Hudson scales~\cite{BH00}) need $N\sim x^{1/2}$ certified
zeros and a parallel window sieve --- the regime where analytic $\pi(x)$
became competitive. A rigorous $S_1(10^{22})$ cross-checked against an
independent combinatorial run is the natural flagship target.
